# IB Diploma Programme results 2004-2026: the worldwide series

Prepared 2026-11-02 from search-engine snippets of the IB's own statistical
bulletins and press releases (ibo.org itself was unreachable from this
session) plus one third-party transcription of the May/Nov 2024 bulletins.
Data file: `ib_session_stats.csv`. Every number below is traceable to the
source named in that file's `source_url`/`note` columns; nothing is from
model memory unless marked UNVERIFIED.

What it shows:

- **Fifteen years inside a one-point band.** From May 2005 to May 2019 the
  worldwide mean total score of all diploma candidates never left the
  29.5-30.1 interval (30.09 in 2005, 29.56 in 2007, 30.07 in 2016, 29.65 in
  2019). The pass rate stayed between 77.8 % and 81.4 %, and the IB said so
  itself: Director General Jeff Beard in July 2011 - "pass rates remain
  consistent year-on-year and are not subject to the grade inflation trends
  seen with other educational systems"; the IB's 2013 results blog - "pass
  rates for the diploma have remained stable over the past five years".
- **Top scores were rare and roughly constant in share.** 108 (2013), 160
  (2015), 146 (2016), 218 (2017), 259 (2018), 275 (2019) candidates scored
  45/45 against a diploma-candidate population of ~80-90,000 (c. 0.1-0.3 %).
  The growth in count tracks cohort growth, not grade drift.
- **The pandemic bump is the exception that proves the rule.** May 2020
  (no exams; grades built from coursework, predicted grades and historical
  school data, then adjusted upward after mass review requests) lifted the
  final mean to 31.34 and the pass rate to 85.18 %; May 2021 (dual route,
  exam or non-exam, with teacher evidence) gave 33.02 and 88.96 % with
  1,351 perfect scores, ~5x the pre-pandemic count; May 2022 (exams with
  deliberately generous boundaries) gave 32.00 and 86.11 %.
- **Then an explicit, deliberate return.** In May 2023 the IB wrote that
  "in line with other national and international awarding organizations,
  the profile of grade distribution returned close to pre-pandemic
  standards": 30.24 points, pass rate ~80 %, share of diploma candidates
  at 40-45 points down from 18.4 % (2021) to 8.9 %. The May 2020 provisional
  bulletin had already stated that "the May 2020 data will not form part of
  the ongoing time series of data which the IB uses to maintain standards".
- **2023-2026 drift upward of ~0.6 points** (30.24 -> 30.32 -> 30.58 ->
  30.88) with the pass rate rising 80 -> 82.6 %. This is the one period that
  an opponent could call inflation; the IB has not (in anything found here)
  explained it. Candidates to check against the bulletins: changing cohort
  mix (India, China, Hong Kong growing; the US, with a 26.59 mean in 2026,
  shrinking in share), the May 2026 Non-Exam Contingency Measure for
  conflict-affected Middle East students, and the new mathematics and
  language courses.

Mean grade (1-7) moves in lock-step: 4.78 (2005), 4.74 (2006), 4.8 (2023),
4.9 (2024), with the pandemic years at 5.1-5.3.

For candidates who were actually awarded the diploma (the population a
Norwegian conversion table applies to) the mean was 32.15 (2012), 32.19
(2013), 32.23 (2019), 32.30 (2020), 33.15 (2021) - i.e. roughly 2.5 points
above the all-candidate mean, and equally flat outside the pandemic.

## 2. How the IB awards grades and why the series is flat

The IB's own research summary on "statistically recommended boundaries"
describes the mechanism: each session, subject grade boundaries are set by
examiner judgement supported by statistical estimates of "the minimum mark at
which each grade is awarded", designed "to account for any change in cohort
ability over time", with prior attainment cited as the stronger anchor. It is
a criterion-related system with standards carried forward year to year, not
a norm-referenced quota and not a teacher-set grade. Diploma total = sum of
six subject grades (max 42) + up to 3 core points, max 45; a pass needs 24
points and no failing conditions. Because all six grades are external or
externally moderated and anchored to previous sessions, the mean cannot
drift the way school-set grades can.

Regime changes to annotate on the chart:

| Session | Regime | IB explanation |
|---|---|---|
| May 2020 | Exams cancelled worldwide; "assessment model" of coursework + predicted grades + historical data; upward adjustment after complaints | IB letter May 2020; results later "adjusted ... using predicted grades" (Gulf News) |
| May 2021 | Dual route: exam route where exams could be held, non-exam route elsewhere | IB release July 2021: >170,000 students; 33.02 / 88.96 % |
| May 2022 | Exams everywhere, boundaries "adapted" for disruption (UNVERIFIED wording - model memory; the data show 32.00 / 86.11 %) | final bulletin |
| May 2023 | Return to pre-pandemic grading profile; 45-point counts dropped from the bulletin | IB release July 2023; bulletin note |
| May 2024-25 | Normal sessions; provisional vs final bulletins differ by ~0.5 pp in pass rate | bulletins |
| May 2026 | Normal, except Non-Exam Contingency Measure for some Middle East schools (coursework + predicted grades) | IB release July 2026 |

## 3. Caveats for the chart

1. **Two populations.** "Mean total points" in the bulletins covers all
   diploma(-and-retake) candidates including those who failed; the mean of
   those awarded the diploma is ~2.5 points higher. Use one consistently.
2. **Pass-rate definitions changed.** Old bulletins (<=2019) report 77-79 %;
   the Power BI bulletins (2020+) recompute earlier years on a different base
   (e.g. 2023: 80.01 % final vs 79.35 % at results day; 2022: 86.11 % vs 81.39 %
   on Clastify). Pandemic-era pass rates from the two generations are not
   strictly comparable; the mean total points is the safer series.
3. **Provisional vs final.** Results-day press figures (29.62 for 2019, 29.92
   for 2020, 31.98 for 2022) differ from final bulletins (29.65, 31.34, 32.00)
   after enquiries-upon-results and, in 2020, the review-driven re-award.
   2026 is provisional only.
4. **Cohort mix.** Diploma candidates grew from ~1,582 schools (2009) to
   3,323 schools / 152 countries (2024), with Asia-Pacific (mean ~33-37 in
   Hong Kong, India 32.78 in 2026) growing and the Americas (US mean 26.59)
   large. A flat worldwide mean can mask opposite drifts by region; the
   bulletins' regional tables should be checked.
5. **Retakes and course candidates** are separate categories; "candidates"
   in the CSV mixes bulletin totals (all categories) with press-release
   "students receiving results" - see notes column.
6. **Bilingual diplomas** (25-28 % of diplomas 2020-24) do not affect the
   point total.
7. **Chart readings.** Values for 2010, 2011, 2014 and 2015 are read from
   unlabeled 5-year bulletin charts in snippets and must be checked against
   the PDFs listed in `TO-FETCH.md`.
8. **Norway.** No Norway-specific IB statistics were found; bulletins only
   break down by IB region. Denmark's 2017 IB conversion table (uvm.dk) is a
   Nordic precedent worth citing.
