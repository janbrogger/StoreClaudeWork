# Norwegian IB conversion table ("omregningstabell/omrekningstabell for IB"): history, politics and debate

Narrative review prepared 2 Nov 2026 for an Aftenposten op-ed. Method: WebSearch
only (all primary hosts except github/pypi were blocked; the per-turn search
budget ran out after ~35 queries, so some items could not be followed up —
see "Gaps"). Every factual statement below is traceable to a search-result
snippet listed under "Sources"; snippet-level reading means wording may be
paraphrased by the search engine. Items from model memory are marked
**UNVERIFIED (model memory)**. Quotes in Norwegian are reproduced as they
appeared in snippets.

## 1. Timeline of the IB conversion table in Norway

### 1.1 The old ("gjeldende") table — in force since the 1990s

* Responsibility: In 2010 Samordna opptak (SO) was given authority by
  Kunnskapsdepartementet to draw up conversion tables for foreign
  qualifications — *except* for the French baccalauréat and the IB.
  Minister Oddmund Hoel (Sp), answer to Abid Raja, 13 Dec 2024:
  > «I 2010 fekk Samordna opptak (SO) myndigheit frå Kunnskapsdepartementet
  > til å utarbeide omrekningstabellar, unntatt for fransk baccalauréat
  > (fransk bacc) og International Baccalaureate (IB).»
  > «Omrekningstabellar blir utarbeida i samband med opptak til høgare
  > utdanning og ligg under ansvarsområdet til forskings- og høgare
  > utdanningsministeren.»
  HK-dir (Direktoratet for høyere utdanning og kompetanse) now runs
  Samordna opptak and produces all tables: «HK-dir forvalter i dag Samordna
  opptak og utarbeider alle omrekningstabellar» (Storting written answer).
* Age of the table: Hoel (answer to Jønnes, 4 Oct 2024) said the current IB
  table has been largely unchanged since the 1990s, with changes made only
  where IB itself altered how it calculates totals. The 29 Oct 2026 press
  release says: «Gjeldende omregningstabell for IB har vært uendret i flere
  tiår. Tabellen er ikke etterprøvbar, og det er uklart hva den er basert
  på.»
* Content of the old table (partial, from snippets):
  - 45 (max) IB points → average 6.0 = 60 karakterpoeng. A school FAQ
    (Kristiansand katedralskole Gimle): «43-45 IB-poeng tilsvarer 60 poeng
    eller 6 i snitt» — i.e. the old table saturates at 6.0 from 43 points.
  - Legeforeningen's attachment ("vedlegg omrekningstabell for IB") compares
    the 2026 proposal with the «original» table for 20–45 points: 20 points
    = 2.6 (original) vs 2.04 (new); 45 = 6.00 in the new column; 43, 44 and
    45 = 6 in the original column.
  - The 2026 høringsnotat annex ("IBs poengskala, dagens tabell og forslag
    til ny tabell") was summarised by the search engine as: IB 20 → 26.0
    (dagens tabell) / 29.5 (forslag); IB 21 → 28.0 / 31.7. **These numbers
    conflict with Legeforeningen's reading (20 → 2.04). The annex has
    several columns (possibly the 2024 global-data proposal and the 2026
    Norway-data proposal) and must be read in full — see TO-FETCH.**
  - Under the 2022 HK-dir table: 18 IB points → 2.44 (below the threshold
    for generell studiekompetanse, Utdanningsnytt); 24 IB points (the
    diploma pass mark) → «karakteren 2 i alle fag» (Dok 8:76 S).

### 1.2 December 2022: HK-dir's new table (to apply from 2025/26 admission)

* 15 Dec 2022: HK-dir published a new conversion table for the IB Diploma
  (and IB Diploma Course Results), to be used for applications to the
  2025/26 academic year onwards (Bergen Katedralskole news page; Samordna
  opptak notice reproduced on the Foreign Ministry's "Familieportalen":
  «Samordna opptak – Tabell som skal gjelde fra studieåret 2025/2026»).
  The notice said SO had received official grade statistics from the IBO
  and that the table would replace the old one for all qualified IB
  applicants regardless of graduation year.
* Method: the same statistical method HK-dir uses for its roughly one
  hundred other conversion tables, developed/assessed by Norsk
  Regnesentral (NR); it compares the grade distribution of the foreign
  system with the Norwegian one (Hoel's written answers). Data: global IBO
  statistics for the last ten years, all Total Points; the 2022/2024
  version started at 24 points (høringsnotat 2026). HK-dir argued that
  using only Norwegian IB-school statistics would give a much smaller
  sample (framlegg 2026).
* Government line in the Storting (Hoel, Oct/Dec 2024): the table is based
  on official IB grade statistics; Sweden and Denmark use very similar
  tables.
* Reception: strong opposition from NIBS, the IBO, Abelia,
  Elevorganisasjonen and ANSA (sections 2–3). Dok 8:76 S: the table «ikke
  ser ut til å ta hensyn til forskjellen i relativ karakterfordeling mellom
  IB-elever og elever fra norsk videregående».

### 1.3 2024: first ministry hearing of HK-dir's table

* The ministry sent HK-dir's table on hearing in 2024 as part of
  «Høyring av forslag om endringar i forskrift om opptak til høgare
  utdanning for opptaksåret 2025-2026» (regjeringen.no id3041173). Abelia
  submitted a response (attachment on that page). The 2026 høringsnotat:
  «HK-dir utarbeidde først ein tabell som vart send på høyring frå
  departementet i 2024. Denne tabellen nytta data frå IB-stiftinga med
  poeng frå diplomresultata hos alle IB-skular i verda.»

### 1.4 Autumn 2024 – winter 2025: Storting activity

* 1 Oct 2024 — Kari-Anne Jønnes (H), written question Dok 15:2 (2024-2025),
  qnid=98049, to the research and higher-education minister: can the
  minister confirm that the new IB table reflects IB students' actual
  knowledge level? Justification: IB schools fear for IB's future in
  Norway; HK-dir chose to disregard national statistics on admission
  points, completion and "expectation indicators" for Norwegian IB
  students; the question also states that 20–25 % of IB pupils in Norwegian
  public schools fail the exam (claim by the questioner, not verified).
  Answered 4 Oct 2024 by Oddmund Hoel (Sp): HK-dir announced the table in
  2022, it takes effect 2025-26, it is based on official IB statistics; the
  current table is largely unchanged since the 1990s.
* 3 Dec 2024 — Abid Raja (V), written question Dok 15:571 (2024-2025),
  qnid=99754, about the new model for converting IB grades; Raja said he had
  received many messages from students, teachers and school leaders.
  Answered 13 Dec 2024 by Hoel (quotes above; ~100 tables, NR method,
  Sweden/Denmark comparison).
* 11 Feb 2025 — Representantforslag Dokument 8:76 S (2024-2025) from
  Kari-Anne Jønnes (H), Himanshu Gulati (FrP), Grete Wold (SV), Abid Raja
  (V) and Kjell Ingolf Ropstad (KrF) «om å utsette endringene i
  omregningstabeller for International Baccalaureate». Proposal text:
  > «Stortinget ber regjeringen utsette endringene i omregningstabeller for
  > International Baccalaureate, som er foreslått gjeldende fra opptaket til
  > studieåret 2025/26, og vente med å fastsette en ny forskriftsendring til
  > tilstrekkelig involvering av berørte parter er ivaretatt.»
  Rationale: «Det har oppstått en sterk bekymring for fremtiden til
  International Baccalaureate (IB) i Norge på grunn av en ny
  konverteringstabell som innføres fra og med opptaksåret 2025–2026.»
  NIBS reports a drop in pupil numbers, which it links to the conversion;
  example of 24 points → grade 2.
* 13 Feb 2025 — the proposal was listed as item 166 in the Storting record;
  a member proposed referral to utdannings- og forskningskomiteen rather
  than immediate decision («Jeg vil istedenfor foreslå at saken sendes til
  utdannings- og forskningskomiteen»). **The committee recommendation
  (Innst.) and the final vote were not found — see Gaps.**

### 1.5 February 2025: Minister Aasland postpones; table withdrawn

* Feb 2025 — Forsknings- og høyere utdanningsminister Sigrun Aasland (Ap)
  stopped the introduction of the table for the 2025 admission, postponed
  it one year (to the 2026 admission) and sent the system on a new hearing
  «for å sikre en bred prosess med innspill fra berørte parter i sektoren»
  (Khrono, «Aasland utsetter omstridt omregning», article 944452). Khrono:
  the table gave lower Norwegian grades for IB results than before; critics
  said the conversion is done statistically and «tar ikke hensyn til den
  store forskjellen i vanskelighetsgrad mellom linjene». ANSA welcomed the
  delay and asked that the conversion not be «unødvendig streng».
* Abelia: «Utsetter urettferdig omregningstabell» (abelia.no, Feb 2025).
  St. Olav vgs (Sarpsborg): «Ny omregningstabell for IB utsatt».
  Utdanningsnytt: «Elevene ble hørt: Ny poengberegning på IB-linja innføres
  ikke likevel» — Samordna opptak withdrew the table so it would not be used
  in 2025 either; a new hearing on IB conversion was to follow.
* Winter 2025 — new hearing: Khrono (952651) reported the government sent on
  hearing a new IB table made with the same statistical method as other
  tables, proposed to apply from the 2026/2027 admission. The 2026 press
  release: «Forslag til ny omregningstabell for IB ble sist sendt på høring
  vinteren 2025.» Many objections came in and the ministry chose not to
  introduce that table (press release / høringsnotat 2026).
* 11 Sep 2025 — new forskrift om opptak til høgare utdanning (nr. 1850)
  adopted; enters into force 1 Jan 2027 and applies from the 2027–2028
  academic year (rundskriv F-01-25; høringsnotat 2026).
* 27 Oct 2025 (Monday) — NIBS and Abelia met Kunnskapsdepartementet for an
  update; NIBS «will be invited to be a part of this process, in
  collaboration with HK-dir»; both sides «agreed that all students in
  Norway should be treated fairly» (Bergen Katedralskole news «Meeting with
  the Ministry of Education»).

### 1.6 29 October 2026: the current hearing

* 26 Oct 2026 — høringsnotat dated; 29 Oct 2026 — press release «Ny
  omregningstabell for IB-karakterer sendes på høring» (regjeringen.no
  id3184606), hearing page id3183537 («Høyring - forslag om endring i
  forskrift om opptak til høgare utdanning - ny omrekningstabell for
  International Baccalaureate», ref. 26/5123), NTB distribution titled «Ny
  omregning for IB-diplom vil gi likebehandling ved opptak til høyere
  utdanning» (kommunikasjon.ntb.no/pressemelding/17950602). Hearing deadline
  10 Dec 2026. Minister: Eileen Fugelsnes (Ap), forsknings- og høyere
  utdanningsminister.
* Government arguments (verbatim where snippets allowed):
  - «Regjeringen vil innføre en ny tabell for omregning av poengsummen fra
    IB-diplom til norske karakterer.»
  - The current method «har vist seg å gi IB-elever en fordel» over other
    applicants; the proposal rests on «en kjent statistisk metode» and is
    said to be fairer and verifiable.
  - «Gjeldende omregningstabell for IB har vært uendret i flere tiår.
    Tabellen er ikke etterprøvbar, og det er uklart hva den er basert på.»
  - HK-dir statistic: «0,08 % av søkerne med norsk videregående opplæring
    (studieforberedende) fikk snitt 6,0 i opptaket, mens 2,8 % av
    IB-søkerne fikk dette.»
  - Rationale: all pupils should «stille likt» when applying for higher
    education (likebehandling).
* Content of the proposal (høringsnotat + «Framlegg til ny omrekningstabell
  for International Baccalaureate (IB)»):
  - «Departementet foreslår å innføre ein ny omrekningstabell for
    International Baccalaureate frå og med opptak til studieåret
    2030-2031.»
  - «Departementet foreslår at ein ny omrekningstabell for IB skal starte
    på 20 IB-poeng og berre vere utarbeida med data frå IB-liner i Noreg.»
    (The 2024 version used global IBO data and started at 24.)
  - A linear conversion for IB points 20–23; HK-dir compared IB statistics
    for all Total Points 20–45 with pupils in Norway who obtain a diploma.
  - HK-dir applied the same statistical method it uses for other tables
    when weighing the objections from NIBS and Abelia.
  - Exception clause for applicants without a full diploma who hold IB
    Diploma Programme Course Results.
  - Applies only to admission in Norway.
* Significance of the dates: the table is proposed to apply from the
  2030–2031 admission, i.e. to pupils who start IB in autumn 2028 — a
  five-year transition compared with the 2022 attempt (announced Dec 2022
  for 2025 admission, hitting pupils already enrolled).

## 2. NIBS — Norwegian IB Schools — and Gillian Boniface

* Identity: NIBS (norwegianibschoolsnibs.org) is the network/association of
  IB World Schools in Norway, founded in 2011; recognised by the IB as an
  «Association of IB World Schools» in 2019; hosts an annual meeting of
  coordinators and school leaders; «represents IB World Schools in
  discussions affecting their interests» and «serves as a resource for
  schools interested in establishing an IB programme» (Trondheim
  International School, Wikipedia). USN describes NIBS as a referring body
  on accreditation of IB students' qualifications within the national
  system and their admission to Norwegian universities and colleges.
* Member schools: not retrieved (site not indexed in snippets). Known IB DP
  schools appearing in sources: Bergen Katedralskole (IB-authorised 1988),
  Kristiansand katedralskole Gimle, Ås vgs (Akershus), St. Olav vgs
  (Sarpsborg), Trondheim International School (MYP/PYP). International
  School of Bergen offers PYP and MYP and «no longer offers the Diploma
  Programme» (Wikipedia). **UNVERIFIED (model memory):** other DP schools
  include Oslo International School, Stavanger (St. Olav vgs Stavanger/
  International School of Stavanger), Kongsberg vgs, Trondheim katedralskole,
  Red Cross Nordic UWC, Ullern vgs, Sandvika vgs, Drammen vgs, Lillestrøm
  vgs, Kristiansund, Tromsø — verify on the NIBS site.
* Letters and campaigns:
  - «Ny tabell for utregning av konkurransepoengsum for IB Diploma», NIBS
    letter to parents and alumni, 3 May 2023 (Norwegian and English
    versions hosted by Bergen Katedralskole and Ås vgs). Positions: the
    table is unfair to IB students and devalues the IB Diploma relative to
    the Norwegian vitnemål; it questions the mathematical model's
    assumptions and says possible sampling bias works against IB students;
    complains that HK-dir «ikke involverte skoleeiere eller organisasjoner
    for IB-skoler i prosessen»; asks recipients to support a revision.
    NIBS also wrote to HK-dir, the ministry and the Storting committee, and
    the IBO Director General sent a separate letter to HK-dir backing the
    concerns.
  - NIBS supplied the enrolment-decline data cited in Dok 8:76 S (2025):
    «NIBS mener at innføringen av ny konverteringstabell fra 2025 skaper
    usikkerhet, og at elever velger bort IB-tilbudet.»
  - Oct 2025: NIBS + Abelia meeting with the ministry (above).
  - Abelia (NHO's association, which organises private schools) is NIBS's
    main ally in the lobbying; it filed a hearing response in 2024 and
    called the table «urettferdig» (Feb 2025).
* Gillian Boniface: the IBO school directory lists «Gillian Mary Boniface»
  as IB Diploma Programme coordinator at Bergen Katedralskole; the NIBS
  board page lists «Gillian Boniface» as «DP Board Representative» for
  Bergen Katedralskole. No statements by her were found in snippets. (A
  2026 UK obituary for a different Gillian Mary Boniface is unrelated.)

## 4. Academic / official analyses of IB performance

* IB research: «The International Baccalaureate Diploma Programme:
  alignment with Norwegian upper secondary education» (UK NARIC for the
  IBO; cover dated Sept 2018, published 2019). Compared the DP with the
  Vitnemål for videregående opplæring in three phases (desk review,
  comparative analysis, synthesis). Findings: strong match of broad aims
  and holistic development goals; maths and science courses show similar
  skills and content with the DP offering wider breadth; IB World Schools in
  Norway are well placed to deliver the DP within the Norwegian system.
  (Alignment study, not an outcomes study.)
* HK-dir/SO statistic (2026): 2.8 % of IB applicants had average 6.0 vs
  0.08 % of Norwegian studieforberedende applicants. This is a comparison
  of *converted* averages under the old table and is the core empirical
  claim behind "advantage". It says nothing about later performance.
* Jønnes's question (Oct 2024) asserts HK-dir disregarded national data on
  admission points, completion and expectation indicators for Norwegian IB
  students — implying such data exist (likely from the hearing material).
* Nothing from NIFU, NOKUT, SSB or universities on IB graduates' later
  performance was found. **UNVERIFIED (model memory):** IBO-commissioned
  studies in the UK (HESA data) and Australia show DP graduates with
  higher completion/first-class rates; no Norwegian equivalent is known.

## 5. Admission context: medicine thresholds and what 45 IB points yield

* Poenggrenser, medicine, UiO (Oslo, autumn intake): ordinary quota 70.2
  (2023), 69.7 (2024), 69.6 (2025), 69.0 (2026); førstegangsvitnemål quota
  61.2 (2023), 60.7 (2024), 60.5 (2025), 60.0 (2026). Spring intake:
  ordinary 69.2 (2023), 68.4 (2024), 68.1 (2025), 67.5 (2026); FGV 60.1
  (2023), 59.0 (2024), 58.1 (2025). (UiO Medical Faculty pages 2024/2025/
  2026, Uniforum, Studentkompasset; small discrepancies between UiO pages
  noted in snippets.) Earlier: 2020/21 FGV 61.6–62.0. UiB, NTNU, UiT
  figures not retrieved (search budget).
* Quota rules: 50 % of places go to førstegangsvitnemål (no age/extra
  points); the ordinary quota adds alderspoeng and tilleggspoeng, hence the
  ~9-point gap between the two thresholds.
* What a 45-point diploma converts to:
  - Old table: 45 → 6.0 = 60 karakterpoeng; 43 and 44 also 6.0 (school
    FAQ; Legeforeningen annex). 60 points sits just below the UiO
    førstegangsvitnemål threshold (60.0–61.2); realfagspoeng/språkpoeng
    (max 4) and, in the ordinary quota, alderspoeng decide the rest.
    **UNVERIFIED (model memory):** how realfagspoeng are credited for IB HL
    science/maths.
  - 2026 proposal: 45 → 6.00 (Legeforeningen annex), so the maximum is
    unchanged, but 43–44 (and everything below) convert lower than before
    — the compression is removed and the curve steepens. The exact values
    for 38–44 (the range of most medicine-bound IB applicants) must be
    read from the «Framlegg»/annex PDFs (TO-FETCH).
  - 2022/2024 HK-dir table: not retrieved for the top range; the bottom was
    24 → 2.0 and 18 → 2.44 (contradictory readings — the 2022 table
    started at 24, so 18 → 2.44 may refer to course results).

## What this means for the op-ed

**The authorities' case (as stated in 2024–2026):**
1. Likebehandling: everyone should «stille likt»; the old table is decades
   old, «ikke etterprøvbar», origin unknown.
2. Empirical: 2.8 % of IB applicants vs 0.08 % of Norwegian applicants
   reach 6.0 — the old table over-rewards the top.
3. Method: one «kjent statistisk metode» (Norsk Regnesentral) used for ~100
   tables; Sweden and Denmark do likewise; now using Norwegian IB data only
   and a long lead time (2030–31).

**Counter-arguments already on record:**
1. Distribution ≠ difficulty: a percentile-matching method assumes IB and
   Norwegian cohorts are equally able; IB pupils are self-selected and the
   programme is harder, so equal percentile mapping is not equal treatment
   (NIBS, Elevorganisasjonen, Khrono critics, Dok 8:76 S).
2. The 2.8 % vs 0.08 % figure is consistent with either (a) an overly
   generous table or (b) a selected cohort, or (c) that the Norwegian 6.0
   is rare because Norwegian grades are capped by teacher-set standpunkt
   and partly randomised exam draws — the statistic alone cannot
   distinguish. (Op-ed opening for the grade-inflation thesis: if Norwegian
   averages have drifted upward since the 1990s while IB totals are
   anchored by external exams, a percentile mapping imports Norwegian
   inflation into the IB conversion.)
3. Sampling bias and small n when using only Norwegian IB-line data (HK-dir
   itself warned of this in 2024; now reversed).
4. Process: 2022 table announced without consulting school owners/NIBS;
   Storting cross-party pressure (H, FrP, SV, V, KrF) and ministerial
   retreat in 2025 show the first attempt failed on process, not only
   substance.
5. Consequences: NIBS-reported enrolment decline; Elevorganisasjonen's
   6 000 signatures; threat to IB's existence in public schools.
6. The 24-point/grade-2 anomaly: an IB pass is treated as a bare Norwegian
   pass.

**Gaps the op-ed team should close (see TO-FETCH.md):**
* The actual 2026 table values 24–45 and the 2024 table for comparison.
* Innstilling and vote on Dok 8:76 S (2024-2025).
* Hearing responses (2024 and winter 2025): UiO/UiB/NTNU, NOKUT,
  Legeforeningen, NIBS, Elevorganisasjonen, ANSA — and Legeforeningen's
  stance in 2026.
* NIBS member list and any NIBS statement on the 29 Oct 2026 proposal.
* Mainstream-media coverage (VG, BT, Aftenposten, NRK) — none surfaced.
* Any Norwegian outcomes data on IB graduates in higher education.
* HK-dir's method note (Norsk Regnesentral) to check the percentile
  assumption.
